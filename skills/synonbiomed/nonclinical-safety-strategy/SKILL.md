# Nonclinical Safety Strategy

Tie every study to a clinical exposure, duration, population, route, or identified risk.

## Scope

Confirm modality, indication, clinical route, schedule, duration, population, starting-dose approach, pharmacology, off-target risks, metabolites, impurities, excipients, and regional plan. Identify product-specific guidance before using a general package.

## Workflow

1. Inventory pharmacology, PK/TK, general toxicity, genotoxicity, safety pharmacology, reproductive and developmental toxicity, carcinogenicity, immunotoxicity, local tolerance, photosafety, juvenile, and abuse-liability evidence.
2. Select relevant species and models using pharmacologic relevance and exposure justification.
3. Align study duration and timing with the proposed clinical program.
4. Define GLP status, dose rationale, endpoints, recovery groups, toxicokinetics, pathology, and data-integrity expectations.
5. Integrate target-organ findings, reversibility, exposure margins, class effects, and human monitoring implications.
6. Record waivers, deferrals, alternative methods, 3R considerations, and authority interactions.
7. Produce a gap and risk assessment with clear decision gates.

## Deliver

Provide a study matrix, timeline, evidence gaps, exposure-margin summary, clinical monitoring implications, and questions for toxicology and regulatory review.

Do not infer safety from an absent signal, a single model, or a non-GLP screen. Mark species relevance, study limitations, and unresolved human risk explicitly.

## Source authorities

Use [ICH M3 and multidisciplinary guidance](https://admin.ich.org/page/multidisciplinary-guidelines) plus the current [ICH Safety guidelines](https://admin.ich.org/page/safety-guidelines), selecting product-specific S-guidelines such as S6, S9, S11, S12, or S13 when applicable.
