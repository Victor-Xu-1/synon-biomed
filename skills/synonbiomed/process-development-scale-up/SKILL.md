# Process Development and Scale-Up

Translate product requirements into a controlled, reproducible manufacturing process.

Classify each input as measured at the stated scale, supported by a cited source, calculated, assumed, or missing. Preserve compound identity, step stoichiometry, isolated yield, impurity identity, equipment geometry, and scale as separate evidence fields. Do not convert laboratory observations or class knowledge into claimed plant-scale performance.

## Workflow

1. Define product and process CQAs, material attributes, process outputs, scale, equipment, site, and commercial demand.
2. Map unit operations, material flows, hold times, recycle or rework, utilities, and contamination controls.
3. Identify scale-dependent mechanisms and failure modes using first principles, prior knowledge, and designed studies.
4. Establish CPPs, proven acceptable ranges or design space, sampling, in-process controls, and material controls.
5. Assess impurity fate and purge, mass and energy balance, mixing, heat and mass transfer, particle engineering, and process safety where relevant.
6. Plan engineering, demonstration, registration, and validation batches with explicit learning objectives.
7. Transfer knowledge through process descriptions, control strategy, batch records, method readiness, training, comparability, and deviation handling.
8. Define continued process verification and change-management signals.

For quantitative or safety-critical work:

- Build explicit mass and molar balances from the supplied batch basis. Run stoichiometry, yield, concentration, solvent volume, throughput, heat-load, gas-evolution, and scale-factor calculations in an admitted calculation environment and independently check units, signs, limiting reagent, closure, and order of magnitude.
- Do not invent operating ranges, addition times, temperatures, agitation rates, heat-transfer coefficients, impurity purge factors, equipment limits, exposure limits, acceptance criteria, or batch counts. When data are absent, define how the parameter will be measured and how its decision limit will be set.
- Keep observed facts, mechanistic hypotheses, development proposals, and qualified-function decisions visibly distinct.
- Trace every proposed control to a failure mode, detection method, decision criterion-setting method, and fallback response. A generic control list is not a control strategy.
- Treat reaction calorimetry, thermal stability, gas evolution, pressure, incompatible chemistry, potent-compound containment, and waste compatibility as evidence needs. Never claim a route is safe or scalable without the applicable measurements.
- Use primary regulatory guidance for regulatory framing and primary literature or verified process data for chemical or engineering claims. Cite the exact public source near the claim; guidance alignment is not proof of compliance.

## Deliver

Provide a process map, risk assessment, scale-up basis, study plan, parameter/CQA matrix, technology-transfer package outline, validation strategy, and unresolved risks. Preserve substantial balances, risk registers, and parameter matrices as usable structured files alongside a readable report. Every ranked recommendation must state its evidence, uncertainty, discriminating experiment, and change trigger.

Before delivery, read back every saved output. Recalculate representative balances independently, confirm that repeated values agree across files, verify that cited sources support nearby claims, and ensure the final recommendation does not depend on an unstated yield, purge, equipment, or safety fact.

Do not assume geometric scale-up preserves performance. Escalate process safety, containment, GMP, and occupational hazards to qualified functions.
