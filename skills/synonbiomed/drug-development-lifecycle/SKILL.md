# Drug Development Lifecycle

Build one traceable development plan rather than independent discipline checklists.

## Frame the program

Establish the product modality, indication, target population, route, dosage form, geography, current phase, ownership, timing constraints, and intended regulatory pathway. Create a target product profile with measurable claims and explicitly mark unknowns.

## Build the lifecycle map

Map evidence and decisions across:

1. Disease biology, target validation, candidate generation, and medicinal chemistry.
2. DMPK, translational pharmacology, bioanalysis, and nonclinical safety.
3. Drug substance, formulation, analytical methods, manufacturing process, stability, and control strategy.
4. Clinical pharmacology, clinical phases, statistics, safety surveillance, and benefit-risk evidence.
5. Regulatory interactions, CTD/eCTD content, inspections, approval, launch, and post-approval commitments.

For each workstream record inputs, accountable function, evidence, dependency, decision criterion, due date, risk, and next action. Distinguish required evidence from optional optimization.

## Route focused work

Use the focused lifecycle skills when depth is needed: medicinal chemistry, DMPK/ADME, nonclinical safety, formulation, analytical methods, process scale-up, CMC, clinical planning, protocol design, biostatistics, regulatory submission, pharmacovigilance, and postmarketing lifecycle management. Preserve one shared target product profile and evidence register across all of them.

## Deliver

Produce:

- an executive lifecycle map;
- a stage-gate decision table;
- an evidence and document inventory;
- a critical-path and dependency view;
- a risk register with mitigation and escalation owners;
- a list of assumptions and items requiring qualified specialist review.

Do not claim regulatory acceptability, clinical safety, manufacturability, or approval from a checklist. Verify current requirements against the intended authority and product type.

## Source authorities

Use the [FDA drug development process](https://www.fda.gov/patients/learn-about-drug-and-device-approvals/drug-development-process) for the lifecycle frame and the current [ICH guideline index](https://admin.ich.org/page/search-index-ich-guidelines) for Quality, Safety, Efficacy, and Multidisciplinary standards.
