# Regulatory Submission Strategy

Treat regulations and guidance as current, jurisdiction-specific evidence.

## Frame

Confirm product type, indication, sponsor, jurisdictions, development phase, desired claims, existing designations, prior authority advice, legal basis, and target filing dates. Separate mandatory requirements, guidance recommendations, precedent, and sponsor strategy.

## Workflow

1. Identify the applicable application and interaction pathway for each jurisdiction.
2. Retrieve current statutes, regulations, guidance, technical standards, templates, and authority notices from official sources.
3. Map nonclinical, clinical, CMC, safety, labeling, pediatric, environmental, data-standard, and administrative evidence to CTD modules.
4. Build a source-document inventory with owner, status, version, approval, data cut, and traceability.
5. Define authority meetings, questions, briefing evidence, decision points, and minutes follow-up.
6. Assess expedited or special pathways without assuming eligibility.
7. Plan eCTD sequence, validation, hyperlinks, study data, publishing, lifecycle, and submission operations.
8. Prepare deficiency scenarios, information-request ownership, response timelines, and change control.

## Deliver

Produce a regulatory roadmap, jurisdiction comparison, CTD/eCTD content map, readiness dashboard, issue log, meeting plan, and decision register.

Do not provide legal advice or claim authority agreement without documented correspondence. Verify current technical requirements immediately before filing.

## Source authorities

Use current [ICH M4/M8 and multidisciplinary guidance](https://admin.ich.org/page/multidisciplinary-guidelines), the FDA [IND resources](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/types-applications/investigational-new-drug-application-ind), and the current FDA [eCTD standards](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/electronic-regulatory-submission-and-review/electronic-common-technical-document-ectd), plus the intended regional authority.
