# Clinical Biostatistics

Start from the clinical question and estimand before choosing a method.

## Workflow

1. Define treatment, population, variable or endpoint, intercurrent-event strategy, and population-level summary.
2. Confirm endpoint definition, assessment schedule, censoring, competing events, and clinically meaningful effect.
3. Specify design, allocation, stratification, analysis sets, covariates, primary model, estimators, confidence intervals, and hypothesis framework.
4. Derive sample size from explicit effect, variability or event rate, alpha, power, allocation, attrition, multiplicity, and operational assumptions.
5. Define missing-data prevention and handling, protocol deviations, estimand-aligned sensitivity analyses, and tipping-point analyses where relevant.
6. Control multiplicity across endpoints, doses, populations, interim looks, and adaptations.
7. Predefine data derivations, tables, listings, figures, validation, blinded review, and reproducibility.
8. Stress-test conclusions under plausible alternative assumptions.

## Deliver

Produce an estimand table, assumptions register, reproducible sample-size calculation, statistical section, SAP outline, multiplicity strategy, missing-data plan, and sensitivity-analysis matrix.

Never fabricate variance, event rates, effect sizes, or dropout. Label illustrative assumptions and show how conclusions change across ranges. Require qualified biostatistical sign-off.

## Source authorities

Use current [ICH E9 and E9(R1), E10, and E20](https://admin.ich.org/page/efficacy-guidelines), plus disease- and endpoint-specific authority guidance.
