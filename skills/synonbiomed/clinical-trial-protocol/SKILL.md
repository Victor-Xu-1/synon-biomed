# Clinical Trial Protocol

Draft only after the scientific question, intervention, population, and decision context are clear.

## Research first

Retrieve comparable trials, published results, current standard of care, product information, authority guidance, and relevant endpoints. Record NCT or registry IDs and links. Distinguish precedent from authority acceptance.

## Protocol workflow

1. Define rationale, objectives, hypotheses, development phase, and estimands.
2. Specify design, arms, controls, randomization, blinding, duration, dose, escalation or adaptation, and stopping criteria.
3. Define population, eligibility, stratification, withdrawal, replacement, and vulnerable-population safeguards.
4. Define primary, secondary, exploratory, safety, PK, biomarker, and patient-reported endpoints with time points and measurement methods.
5. Build the schedule of activities, intervention management, adherence, concomitant medication, rescue therapy, and unblinding rules.
6. Define safety surveillance, adverse-event handling, dose-limiting toxicity, committees, and expedited reporting.
7. Specify sample-size assumptions, analysis sets, missing data, multiplicity, interim analysis, sensitivity analysis, and data standards.
8. Address consent, GCP, data management, monitoring, quality-by-design, sites, supply, laboratories, and archiving.
9. Run consistency checks across synopsis, objectives, endpoints, schedule, estimands, and statistical sections.

## Deliver

Provide a synopsis, complete section outline or draft, schedule-of-activities table, open-issue log, source register, and cross-section consistency report. Use explicit TBD markers instead of inventing facts.

Require medical, statistical, operational, ethics, regulatory, and sponsor review before use.

## Source authorities

Use current [ICH E6, E8, E9, E10, E17, and E20](https://admin.ich.org/page/efficacy-guidelines), ICH M11 when structured protocol requirements apply, and current ClinicalTrials.gov or regional registries for precedent.
