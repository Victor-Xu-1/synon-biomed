# Evidence synthesis and report construction

## Claim-evidence map

Before drafting, represent every decision-relevant claim with:

- its exact scope, population or experimental system, intervention or mechanism, comparator, endpoint and time frame;
- the source record's stable identifier and authoritative locator;
- an exact excerpt or structured response field path from the retrieved version;
- study design, sample size or experimental coverage, uncertainty and relevant limitations when reported;
- its role as support, contradiction, qualification or context;
- whether the statement is an observed result, the source's interpretation, a bounded inference, or an unresolved gap.

Discovery snippets and source counts do not support detailed claims. A patent establishes disclosure, claims, examples and ownership, not clinical efficacy. A trial registry establishes protocol and posted-result facts, not unpublished outcomes. A review supplies framing and citation paths, not primary-result counts.

## Compare evidence rather than count it

Evaluate directness and validity within each scientific field. Explain disagreement using population, model, intervention, formulation, dose, assay, comparator, endpoint, timing, statistical uncertainty or source limitations. Keep unresolved conflicts visible and reduce confidence accordingly.

Avoid mechanical scores unless the method and every input are reproducible from the ledger. Association scores, search ranks, citation counts and patent-family size are context, not universal confidence measures.

## Compose the report

Organize by the user's decisions or competing explanations. Each major section should begin with the synthesis, then present the strongest direct evidence, independent corroboration, contradicting or limiting evidence, and the resulting confidence. Recommendations should state what evidence supports them, where the inference stops, and what new result would change the ranking.

Generate citations and the reference list from stable identifiers and retrieved source metadata. Do not fill author names, years, study identifiers, trial status, patent status or numerical results from memory. Keep publication date, update date, registry date, priority date and retrieval date distinct.

The editable ledger should distinguish candidate, included and excluded records. Include the retrieval track, eligibility or exclusion reason, evidence role, directness, verification state, source URL, excerpt or structured field path, and limitations. Validate the final CSV with a real CSV parser before saving.

Report missing authoritative evidence as a gap. Do not substitute a nearby source class or weaken the original question merely to make every section look complete.
