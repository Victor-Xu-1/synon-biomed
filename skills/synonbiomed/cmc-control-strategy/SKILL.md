# CMC Control Strategy

Connect development knowledge, manufacturing controls, specifications, stability, and lifecycle management to patient risk.

## Workflow

1. Confirm product modality, phase, route, dosage form, sites, markets, and submission milestone.
2. Build QTPP-to-CQA traceability for drug substance, excipients, drug product, device or container, and microbiological attributes.
3. Map raw-material, starting-material, process, in-process, release, stability, packaging, shipping, and facility controls.
4. Assess impurities, degradants, elemental and mutagenic risks, residuals, adventitious agents, extractables/leachables, and cross-contamination as applicable.
5. Define phase-appropriate specifications and justify tests, methods, limits, and reduced or deferred controls.
6. Integrate formulation, analytical, process, stability, comparability, validation, and supply-chain evidence.
7. Map evidence to CTD quality sections and identify missing reports, source data, approvals, and commitments.
8. Plan post-approval changes, knowledge management, and continued verification.

## Deliver

Produce a CMC readiness matrix, integrated control-strategy table, quality risk register, CTD content map, phase-appropriate gaps, and decision or meeting questions.

Do not equate document presence with acceptable evidence. Verify current regional expectations and identify assumptions requiring quality or regulatory approval.

## Source authorities

Use the current [ICH Quality guideline family](https://admin.ich.org/page/quality-guidelines), [ICH M4 and M8](https://admin.ich.org/page/multidisciplinary-guidelines), and current FDA phase-appropriate [IND CMC information](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/investigational-new-drug-ind-application/ind-applications-clinical-investigations-chemistry-manufacturing-and-control-cmc-information).
